# Axioma

Axioma is a quiet number puzzle about spotting order in a crowded field. Find pairs, clear a path through the board, and see how far your reasoning can take you.

## The Puzzle

Choose two uncrossed numbers that either match or add up to 10:

- Matching pairs: `1 + 1`, `2 + 2`, and so on.
- Sum-to-10 pairs: `1 + 9`, `2 + 8`, `3 + 7`, `4 + 6`, or `5 + 5`.

The pair must be connected by a clear path. Numbers can be matched in the board's left-to-right, top-to-bottom sequence when every number between them has already been crossed out, or vertically in the same column when the numbers between them are clear. A crossed-out number is an empty space, so clearing pairs can open routes to new matches. Diagonal matches are not allowed.

Your aim is to clear the entire field. There may not be a match available at every moment, so decide when to keep searching and when to deal another line.

## Dealing

Use the plus button to deal the uncrossed numbers again at the end of the field. This gives you another chance to make pairs, but adds to the puzzle: each new number costs 10 points. Completely cleared rows disappear, and each row removed earns 100 points. Dealing is unavailable until you have made your first move.

## Score And Stats

Matches earn points, with the score influenced by the pair and the distance between its numbers. The stats panel tracks your score, time, moves, deals, match types, and board size. Clear every number to win and see your run's results.

## Make It Yours

Choose a visual theme from the theme selector. Your game and theme are saved automatically, so you can return to your puzzle later. Use the restart button to begin again; it may ask you to confirm before clearing your current progress.

## Where It Began

The idea started on a plane, when my mom showed me a number-pairing game she used to play at university decades earlier. I played it on paper for three days, filling over two pages before finally winning. Paper had one frustrating flaw: cleared rows stayed on the page, the puzzle kept sprawling, the gap was growing and it became harder to track the numbers that left. A digital version could let cleared space actually disappear.  
  
Axioma grew from that thought.
